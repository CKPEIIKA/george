    About George
    
    
    George computes Gröbner bases of ideals in free associative and in commutative polynomial algebras, degree by degree, and from them Hilbert and Poincaré–Betti series, Anick resolutions and Betti numbers of algebras and modules, and Hochschild homology.
    
    
    George is an interface to bergman, written by Jörgen Backelin with Svetlana Cojocaru, Alexander Podoplelov, Victor Ufnarovski and others, and named after George Bergman, whose diamond lemma underlies the method. George does not reimplement it: it runs bergman-1.001-fix, the original bergman 1.001 with documented fixes and a legacy mode, on ECL, a Common Lisp compiled to WebAssembly, inside your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.
    
    
    Input
    
    
    Relations are sums of monomials with integer coefficients, as in bergman’s own algforminput: 5*x*y, x^2-y^2. In the noncommutative case the order of the factors matters, so x*y-y*x is the commutator. The order of the generators decides the variable order.
    
    
    What can be computed
    
    
    
      
    Gröbner bases
    Noncommutative and commutative, over ℚ and 𝔽p, with degree-lexicographic, reverse lexicographic, elimination and matrix orders, and weighted generators.
    
      
    Series
    Hilbert series of commutative and noncommutative quotients, and the double Poincaré–Betti series.
    
      
    Resolutions
    Anick resolutions, Betti numbers of the trivial module, of right and left modules, of pairs of modules and of factor-algebras, and Hochschild homology.
    
      
    Console
    Lisp forms and file-based bergman commands in a persistent session.
    
    
    
    
    Related
    
    
    Bergman 2 by Samuel Mimram is an independent OCaml implementation of noncommutative Gröbner bases and Anick resolutions in the browser. Its example families are available in George under “Start from”.
